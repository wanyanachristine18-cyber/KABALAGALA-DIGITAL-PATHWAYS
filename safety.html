Online Safety

Use these simple checks to stay safer while searching for training and applying online.

1. How to Check a Website

  • Look at the address carefully. Prefer sites ending in .go.ug, .ac.ug, or well-known organisations.
  • Check for the padlock icon (HTTPS) in the browser.
  • Be careful with sites that ask for money before giving clear information.
  • Search the organisation name + “scam” or “review” before sending money or personal documents.

2. Passwords and Two-Step Login

Strong password habits

  • Use at least 8–12 characters
  • Mix letters, numbers and symbols
  • Do not reuse the same password on many sites
  • Never share your password with anyone

Two-step verification (2FA)

When available, turn on two-step login. The site will send a code to your phone or email when you sign in from a new device. This makes it much harder for someone else to use your account.

3. Scam Warning Signs

Common red flags

  • Someone asks you to pay a fee before you receive training or a job
  • Messages that create extreme urgency (“Apply in the next 1 hour or lose the chance”)
  • Requests for your password, PIN or full bank details
  • Offers that sound too good to be true (very high salary for no experience)
  • Poor spelling and grammar in official-looking messages

4. How to Report or Verify a Claim

  • If you are unsure about a training provider, contact the organisation using a phone number or email from their official website (not from the message you received).
  • For possible scams in Uganda you can also report to the Uganda Communications Commission (UCC) or the police cybercrime unit.
  • Talk to a trusted teacher, mentor or family member before sending money.

When in doubt, stop and verify. It is better to miss one opportunity than to lose money to a scam.
